01 / Featured case study

Nuzzly

Creating one clear system for discovery, booking, messaging, and support.

Discipline
UX writing · Content strategy · QA
Role
UX Writer + Product QA
Year
2026
Product
Two-sided pet-care marketplace

Overview

Nuzzly helps pet owners find sitters and helps sitters manage requests. As I tested the product end to end, I found that the interface often told different stories about the same booking: a confirmation on one screen, a pending state on another, and an unrelated action in messages.

I documented more than 40 UX, content, and release-readiness issues; mapped the owner and sitter journeys; and proposed language patterns that made status, responsibility, and next steps explicit.

The core problem wasn’t isolated “weird text.” Nuzzly needed one source of truth for what had happened, who needed to act, and what came next.

My approach

I combined hands-on QA with content design. I moved through the product in both roles, compared repeated states across Explore, Messages, and the dashboard, and reviewed session behavior in PostHog.

  1. Trace the journey.Follow each booking from search through confirmation, completion, review, and support.
  2. Find contradictions.Compare labels, CTAs, permissions, and empty states across surfaces.
  3. Define the truth.Name each state by what is actually happening—not what the system hopes will happen.
  4. Write the next step.Pair every status or error with the most relevant action.

01 · Booking states

A booking displayed “Booking Confirmed” even while it was still waiting for the sitter. This created a trust problem: the owner could reasonably believe care was secured when it was not.

BeforeBooking Confirmed

The sitter has not confirmed yet.

RecommendedWaiting for sitter confirmation

We’ll let you know when your sitter responds.

I proposed a shared status model so the dashboard, booking details, messages, and notifications would use the same terms. Contact information would appear only after confirmation, and refund language only when a booking was actually eligible.

Content principle

Status + actor + next step: say what is happening, who needs to act, and what the user can expect.

02 · Actions that say what they do

The label “Cancel” appeared where the user might be closing a dialog, abandoning an in-progress request, or cancelling an existing booking. Those are different consequences and need different language.

BeforeCancel

Could dismiss the screen—or cancel the booking.

RecommendedKeep booking

Paired with “Cancel booking” when cancellation is the destructive action.

I also flagged duplicate Message buttons that led to the same conversation. Removing the duplicate reduced competition between identical actions and made the page hierarchy clearer.

03 · Honest empty states

“No active sits yet” sounded like the user had never completed a sit, even when past bookings existed. It confused “nothing active right now” with “no history.”

BeforeNo active sits yet

A dead end that could misrepresent booking history.

RecommendedYou don’t have any active sits right now

Plan your next trip or review a past booking.

I applied the same recovery-first pattern to Explore: explain why no results appeared, preserve the user’s inputs, and offer concrete options such as changing dates or expanding the search area.

04 · Messages and visibility

Applications, unread messages, and reviews could exist without being visible from the dashboard. This was partly an information-architecture issue, but language had to make the new structure scannable.

ApplicationsNeeds answers

Surfaces requests waiting on the host.

MessagesStart the conversation

Turns an empty thread into a clear invitation.

Activity4 days ago

Uses human time instead of an opaque timestamp.

I recommended role-specific dashboards so owners see trips and booking actions while sitters see listings, applications, and hosting tasks. A missing Add trip action was restored to keep the owner journey actionable.

05 · Community and reviews

Community cards and review flows contained vague or missing states. I proposed small, reusable patterns that made participation feel lower-risk and more understandable.

New postBe the first to like

Invites participation without implying existing activity.

Review formClear rating prompts

Makes required feedback and submission status visible.

Incomplete contentNeeds answers

Identifies what requires attention without blaming anyone.

I also flagged review discoverability, modal visibility, favorites without a destination, and report access as connected content-and-navigation problems.

06 · AI to human support

When a user typed “ok connect me,” the assistant did not connect them to support or acknowledge the request. The interface created an expectation of escalation, then went silent.

BeforeNo handoff confirmation

The user could not tell whether anyone would respond.

RecommendedI can connect you with Nuzzly Support.

Your conversation summary will be included so you won’t need to repeat everything.

The proposed handoff sets the destination, explains what information carries over, and offers one explicit action. I also documented the emergency panel covering chat and made uninterrupted access to safety and reporting content a launch requirement.

07 · Onboarding and sign-in

Account flows needed clearer recognition and recovery. I proposed labels such as “Last used” to identify the remembered sign-in method and “Use another email” to give people a visible alternative.

I also reviewed verification mismatches, Welcome Guide placement and printing, guided-tour alignment, and refresh behavior. The goal was consistent: preserve context and never make someone restart because the interface failed to explain itself.

Release criteria

I turned individual findings into reusable product checks:

  • Every booking state has one meaning across every surface.
  • Each page has one primary action—no duplicate CTAs.
  • Empty and error states include a valid recovery path.
  • Role labels, permissions, and dashboards match the current user.
  • Contact, payment, and refund language appears only when applicable.
  • AI escalation clearly confirms the handoff to a person.
  • Reviews, reports, notifications, and safety controls remain discoverable.

Outcome

The work produced a prioritized QA and content audit, flow diagrams, launch-blocker criteria, dashboard and sign-in concepts, and a reusable language system for status, actions, empty states, and support.

40+content and UX issues documented
7connected content areas
1shared model for product states

Because the product had not yet completed a controlled release, I did not attach an unverified conversion metric. I defined PostHog funnels and session-replay checks as the next measurement step.
