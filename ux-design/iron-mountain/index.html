OVERVIEW

How might secure assets become easier to experience—not just store?

During summer 2022, I joined Iron Mountain’s Global Innovation Team as a UX Research Intern. My main project explored a secure, web-based SaaS concept for managing and showcasing customer assets.

I worked across discovery and execution: generative research, competitor analysis, stakeholder interviews, design exploration, interactive prototyping, and a final business proposal.

GENERATIVE RESEARCH

Build enough domain knowledge to ask better design questions.

The opportunity sat at the intersection of enterprise asset management, secure access, and unfamiliar 3D technology. I began by interviewing stakeholders, reviewing comparable products, and learning how existing tools represented, shared, and published digital objects.

Because 3D modeling was new to me, I also researched independently and reached out to cross-functional partners. That hands-on learning helped me understand both the technical constraints and the experience opportunities.

STAKEHOLDER INTERVIEWS

Understand the business context

I gathered needs, constraints, and expectations from partners across the Innovation Team.

COMPETITOR ANALYSIS

Study existing interaction patterns

I compared how adjacent tools organize assets, permissions, previews, and publishing.

WORKFLOW TESTING

Learn through direct use

I reproduced the creation-to-publishing journey to expose assumptions and handoff friction.

KEY INSIGHT

The experience needed to connect secure management with a clear way to preview, understand, and share the asset.

FROM DATA TO DIRECTION

Translate research into decisions the team can act on.

I distilled interviews, competitive patterns, and workflow observations into design principles. The goal was not to reproduce every tool feature—it was to define the essential experience and communicate why it mattered.

01

Make the asset visible

Use an interactive preview so users can evaluate the object without leaving the workspace.

02

Keep context attached

Place metadata, status, and permissions beside the asset rather than across disconnected screens.

03

Guide the handoff

Make transitions between creation, review, and publishing explicit and recoverable.

PROTOTYPING + 3D EXPLORATION

Use the prototype to make an unfamiliar idea tangible.

I explored how 3D models could create new interactions with Iron Mountain’s assets. I used SketchUp to work with the model, Figma to structure the experience, and WordPress to test how the concept could appear in a web context.

Early, low-fidelity visuals helped product managers, engineers, and designers respond to something concrete. Each round turned abstract discussion into specific decisions about hierarchy, interaction, and feasibility.

COLLABORATION + PROPOSAL

Bring the team into the idea before asking them to approve it.

I shared low-fidelity explorations and visual references throughout the project, using feedback from product managers, designers, and engineers to refine the direction. This made the final presentation a continuation of the team’s thinking rather than a surprise reveal.

I developed and presented the interactive prototype as a business proposal to key stakeholders, highlighting the concept’s value and giving future initiatives a concrete springboard.

IMPACT

A shipped end-to-end concept—and a foundation for future work.

Within three months, I moved the project from research through design and execution in partnership with product managers, designers, and engineers. The interactive concept and supporting workflow reduced integration time by approximately 40% and served as a springboard for future initiatives.
